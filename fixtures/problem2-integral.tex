\section*{Problem 2: Integration by Substitution}

Evaluate the definite integral
\[ \int_{0}^{1} \frac{x}{x^{2}+1} \, dx \]
and give the exact value.

\section*{Solution}

\textbf{Step 1.} Substitute $u = x^{2}+1$, so that $du = 2x \, dx$.
The limits change as well: when $x = 0$, $u = 1$; when $x = 1$, $u = 2$.

\textbf{Step 2.} With this substitution the integral becomes
\[
\int_{0}^{1} \frac{x}{x^{2}+1} \, dx
= \frac{1}{2} \int_{1}^{2} \frac{du}{u}.
\]

\textbf{Step 3.} Since the antiderivative of $1/u$ is $\ln|u|$,
\[
\frac{1}{2} \int_{1}^{2} \frac{du}{u}
= \frac{1}{2} \left[ \ln|u| \right]_{1}^{2}
= \frac{1}{2} \ln 2.
\]

\section*{Answer}

\[
\int_{0}^{1} \frac{x}{x^{2}+1} \, dx = \frac{1}{2} \ln 2.
\]
