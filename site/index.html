01 —” The problemAdvanced mathematics has a visual monopoly on its diagrams.

Algebra, topology and category theory run on commutative diagrams —” objects connected by labelled arrows, with conditions that certain paths compose equally. Today those diagrams reach blind and low-vision readers, if at all, as prose descriptions written by a sighted intermediary. The evidence says that is not access: fewer than a third of described mathematical images are understood correctly, and a large share of vision-impaired students simply avoid the graphics altogether —” with real consequences for the fields they choose.

The workaround that actually survives in practice is reading the raw LaTeX source with a screen reader. That proves linear access works —” but it offers no navigation between the parts of a diagram, no overview, and no way to check a commuting condition without working everything out by hand.

02 —” The solutionTreat the diagram as data, not as a picture.

A commutative diagram is already structured information in its source: named objects, labelled arrows between them, and declared path equalities. Oracle builds on that structure instead of fighting the pixels:

Intake

Parse existing tikz-cd and quiver source into a graph of objects and arrows —” no re-authoring, no OCR, no sighted intermediary.

Navigate

Move through the diagram by ear: an overview, then object-by-object and arrow-by-arrow reading, following paths the way the mathematics does.

Verify

Declared commuting conditions are checked computationally, and the answer —” verified, refuted, or unknown —” is reported honestly.

Reading protocol follows the research-backed structure: objects first, then arrows with domains and codomains, then the equalities —” then the reader can ask for path composites or a commutativity check.

04 —” Key featuresDesigned with the mathematics inside.

Source-aware intake

Works from the LaTeX that authors already write, so published material becomes accessible without anyone redrawing it.

Structured speech navigation

An explicit reading protocol: objects first, then arrows with their domains and codomains, then the equalities that make the diagram commute.

Computational checking

Path-equality checks give the reader something prose never can: an answer to "does this square actually commute?"

Screen-reader first

Built for the tools blind readers already use —” the accessibility of the tool itself is the project's credibility test.

Open ecosystem path

Designed to live inside the open mathematics-publishing ecosystem rather than as another walled tool.

No AI in the deliverable

Deterministic parsing and checking —” the reading you hear is the mathematics that is there.
